What each mode does
The driver's R34F6GU.GPD exposes four options: AUTO, SUPERCELL, 6×6, 8×8 (default). Their actual matrices live inside the binary .dll files — this tool uses standard halftone matrices that match what those modes are. SUPERCELL uses Ostromoukhov & Hersch 1999's stochastic clustered-dot algorithm (disk-packing + Voronoi-rank thresholds). Grain Touch is RISO marketing for FM/stochastic screening.

Stochastic Cluster Ostromoukhov '99

About this algorithm
Builds an N×N dither matrix by visiting cells in a chosen order, dropping a disk of radius r at each free cell. Dot centers end up min r / max 2r apart. Each cell's threshold = rank of its distance to the nearest center → dots grow outward from centers as ink density rises. The natural 2:1 dot/Voronoi-vertex ratio gives bigger black dots in highlights, smaller white dots in shadows — same as RISO's supercell mode. Per the paper: r=8 ≈ 150 lpi @ 1200 dpi, larger r improves tone reproduction on high-dot-gain devices (like RISO).
